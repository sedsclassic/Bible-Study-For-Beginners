BibleProject · A short study

Son of Man

A mysterious title from Daniel's vision—the human ruler who receives God's kingdom and reigns through service, suffering, and resurrection.

▶ Watch the video
01

Consider the story

Think about how a strange vision in Daniel becomes the way Jesus explains who he is.

1

In Daniel's vision, violent beasts rise from the sea to rule, and then a human figure arrives on the clouds. What is the vision saying about the difference between beastly power and true human rule?

2

The "son of man" is given authority over every nation by the Ancient of Days. Why does it matter that this ruler is both fully human and receives his kingdom from God?

3

The same vision shows God's holy people suffering before they are vindicated. How does that shape what we expect from the Son of Man?

4

Jesus uses "Son of Man" for himself more than any other title, in moments of service, suffering, and glory. What is he claiming about who he is and how he rules?

02

Reflect on the message

Consider what kind of power Jesus embodies and what it asks of those who follow him.

5

The beasts devour, while the Son of Man rules by serving. Where are you tempted to measure strength by control rather than by service?

6

Jesus says the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve. What would it look like to share in that kind of authority in your relationships this week?

7

Daniel's vision ends with justice and a kingdom that never ends. How does that hope steady you when the powers of this world feel beastly?

One thought to carry with you
Jesus is the true human who receives God's kingdom—not by seizing power like the beasts, but by serving, suffering, and being raised to rule forever.
Keep exploring: Read Daniel 7:1–14, Ezekiel 2:1–3, Mark 10:42–45, Mark 14:61–62, and Matthew 28:18. Notice how Jesus draws on Daniel's vision to describe both his suffering and his rule.

For personal study or group discussion. The questions are open-ended; the aim is thoughtful understanding, not a single perfect answer.

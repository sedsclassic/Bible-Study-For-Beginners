BibleProject · A short study

Exile

The long story of humanity driven from God's presence—from Eden to Babylon—and the promise of a homecoming through Jesus.

▶ Watch the video
01

Consider the story

Think about how the pattern of leaving home and longing to return runs through the whole Bible.

1

Adam and Eve are sent out of Eden after choosing their own way. What does their exile reveal about the cost of turning away from God's presence?

2

The pattern returns with Cain, with Babel, and finally with Israel in Babylon. Why does the Bible keep telling stories of people driven from home and scattered?

3

The prophets explain Israel's exile as the fruit of injustice and idolatry, yet they also promise a return. How do judgment and hope sit side by side in their message?

4

On the cross, Jesus is cast out and bears the consequence of humanity's rebellion. How does this reshape what it means to be brought home?

02

Reflect on the message

Consider what it means to live as someone who is far from home and being led back.

5

Exile is the experience of being displaced from home and from God's presence. When have you felt that way, and what did that season teach you?

6

The New Testament calls believers foreigners and exiles in this world. How might that identity change what you hold tightly and what you hold loosely?

7

God promises a homecoming where he lives with his people again. How might that promise shape the way you wait through painful or unfinished seasons?

One thought to carry with you
Exile is not the end of the story. Jesus enters humanity's exile himself, bearing its consequences, so that those far from God can finally come home.
Keep exploring: Read Genesis 3:22–24, Genesis 11:1–9, Jeremiah 29:10–14, 1 Peter 2:9–12, and Revelation 21:3–4. Notice how the story of being sent away becomes a story of being gathered home.

For personal study or group discussion. The questions are open-ended; the aim is thoughtful understanding, not a single perfect answer.

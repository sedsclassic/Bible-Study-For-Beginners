BibleProject · A short study

Covenantal Relationship

God wants more than a distant relationship with humanity. Through covenants, he invites people into a lasting partnership and remains faithful even when they fail.

▶ Watch the video
01

Consider the covenant story

Think about what makes a covenant different from an ordinary promise and how God develops this relationship throughout the Bible.

1

A covenant is more than a promise; it creates a relationship with commitments and responsibilities. What does this reveal about the kind of relationship God wants with humanity?

2

God makes covenants with Noah, Abraham, Israel, and David. How does each covenant develop God's plan to bring blessing and restoration to the world?

3

Humanity repeatedly breaks its commitments, but God remains faithful. What does this pattern reveal about God's character?

4

God chooses Israel to become a blessing to all nations. Why is this purpose important for understanding the story of the Bible?

02

Reflect on God's faithfulness

Consider how the covenant story points toward Jesus and changes the way we understand our relationship with God.

5

Jeremiah speaks of a new covenant in which God's instruction will be written on people's hearts. How is this different from simply following rules from the outside?

6

Jesus fulfills the covenant story and opens the way for people to be reconciled with God. How does his faithfulness answer humanity's repeated failure?

7

If God invites us into a covenant relationship and partnership, what might it look like to participate in his purposes in everyday life?

One thought to carry with you
The Bible's covenant story reveals a God who desires a lasting relationship with humanity. Even when people are unfaithful, God remains committed to his promise. Through Jesus, the covenant story reaches its fulfillment and invites us to become partners in his work of bringing blessing and restoration to the world.
Keep exploring: Read Genesis 9:8–17, Genesis 12:1–3, Exodus 19:3–6, 2 Samuel 7:8–16, Jeremiah 31:31–34, and Luke 22:19–20. Notice how each covenant builds on the previous one and moves the biblical story toward Jesus.

For personal study or group discussion. The questions are open-ended; the aim is thoughtful understanding, not a single perfect answer.

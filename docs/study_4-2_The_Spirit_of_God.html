BibleProject · A short study

The Spirit of God

The breath of God at work from the first page of creation—renewing the world and partnering with humans to bring life out of chaos.

▶ Watch the video
01

Consider the story

Think about how the Spirit appears in Genesis and how God's renewing work unfolds through the rest of the story.

1

Genesis 1 pictures God's Spirit hovering over the dark, formless waters. What does this image suggest about the Spirit's role in bringing order and life out of chaos?

2

The Hebrew word ruach can mean wind, breath, or spirit. How does this one word help us see the Spirit as both powerful and life-giving?

3

Throughout the Old Testament, the Spirit empowers people to build, lead, and speak for God. What do these stories reveal about how God partners with humans to care for his world?

4

Ezekiel promises that God's Spirit will give new hearts and raise dry bones to life. How do Jesus' baptism and the day of Pentecost show that promise coming true?

02

Reflect on the message

Consider what it means to live with the same Spirit who hovered over creation at the beginning.

5

If the Spirit hovers over chaos to bring forth life, where might the Spirit be at work in the disorder of your own life?

6

God partners with people through his Spirit rather than working around them. What might it look like to join his renewing work this week?

7

Scripture pictures the Spirit as breath, wind, and a gentle dove. Which image speaks most to what you need right now, and why?

One thought to carry with you
The Spirit is not an impersonal force but God's own presence—breathing life into creation from the beginning, and now making his people new from the inside out.
Keep exploring: Read Genesis 1:1–2, Ezekiel 36:24–27, Ezekiel 37:1–14, Luke 3:21–22, and Acts 2:1–21. Notice how the Spirit who hovered over the waters is poured out on God's people.

For personal study or group discussion. The questions are open-ended; the aim is thoughtful understanding, not a single perfect answer.

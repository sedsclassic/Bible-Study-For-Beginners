BibleProject · A short study

Sin

Sin is more than breaking rules. The biblical picture reveals how humans fail to live out their purpose and how that failure damages our relationship with God and one another.

01

Consider the meaning of sin

Explore the biblical meaning of sin and what it reveals about the human condition.

1

The Hebrew word khata can describe missing a target or failing to reach a goal. How does this image expand your understanding of what sin is?

2

If sin is more than breaking rules, what is the deeper purpose that humans are failing to fulfill when they sin?

3

Humans are created to reflect God's character and represent his goodness in the world. How does sin prevent us from living out that calling?

4

Sin affects more than the person committing it; it damages relationships and communities. Why is it difficult to separate our personal choices from their effects on others?

02

Reflect on the human condition

Consider how sin appears in everyday life and what it means to recognize our need for God.

5

We often recognize obvious wrongdoing but overlook quieter forms of selfishness, pride, or indifference. Why might these be just as destructive?

6

If sin means failing to love God and others as we should, how might this change the way we examine our own actions and attitudes?

7

Why is it important to acknowledge sin honestly rather than simply trying to appear like a good person? How does recognizing our failure help us understand our need for God's forgiveness?

One thought to carry with you
Sin is not merely the violation of a rule; it is a failure to live according to God's good purpose for humanity. It distorts our ability to reflect his character, love others, and participate in the life we were created for.
Keep exploring: Read Genesis 1:26–28, Genesis 3:1–13, Romans 3:23–24, Romans 6:20–23, and 1 John 1:8–9. Notice how Scripture connects humanity's purpose, our failure, and God's invitation to forgiveness and restoration.

For personal study or group discussion. The questions are open-ended; the aim is thoughtful understanding, not a single perfect answer.

Sacrifice and Atonement

Why does the Bible connect forgiveness with sacrifice? Explore how atonement addresses sin, restores relationship with God, and points toward Jesus.

▶ Watch the video
01

Consider the meaning of sacrifice

Think about how the sacrificial system addresses the problem of sin and makes restoration possible.

1

In the Bible, sin damages the relationship between God and humanity. What does atonement accomplish that a simple apology or promise to do better cannot?

2

In the sacrificial system, an animal's life is given on behalf of the worshiper. What might this teach us about the seriousness of sin and the value of life?

3

The Bible connects blood with life and describes sacrifice as a means of purification. Why might restoring a relationship with a holy God involve both forgiveness and cleansing?

4

The prophets criticized sacrifices when people continued to practice injustice and ignore God. What does this reveal about the difference between performing a religious ritual and genuinely turning back to God?

02

Reflect on the meaning of atonement

Consider what sacrifice reveals about God's character and how Jesus fulfills this story.

5

Why might sacrifice be understood not as God demanding something out of cruelty, but as God providing a way to deal with the destruction caused by evil?

6

Jesus gives his own life rather than asking someone else to pay the cost. How does this shape your understanding of God's love and the seriousness with which he takes evil?

7

If Jesus has made reconciliation with God possible, what should that change about the way we respond to forgiveness and treat others?

One thought to carry with you
Atonement is God's way of confronting the damage caused by sin and making restoration possible. The sacrificial system points toward Jesus, who gives his own life to deal with evil and bring humanity back into relationship with God.
Keep exploring: Read Leviticus 17:11, Isaiah 53:4–6, Mark 10:45, Romans 3:21–26, and Hebrews 9:11–14. Notice how the Bible develops the connection between life, sacrifice, forgiveness, and the work of Jesus.

For personal study or group discussion. The questions are open-ended; the aim is thoughtful understanding, not a single perfect answer.

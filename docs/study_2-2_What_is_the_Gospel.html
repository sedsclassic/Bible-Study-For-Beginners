The Gospel

Four accounts, one announcement: Jesus is the Messiah, and his death and resurrection change everything.

01

Consider the story

Think about how the Gospel authors present Jesus and connect his story to the bigger biblical narrative.

1

The word "Gospel" means good news, but it announces more than personal salvation. What does it mean to proclaim Jesus as the true King of the world?

2

The Gospel authors connect Jesus' life to the Hebrew Scriptures through quotations, allusions, and parallels. Why is it important to understand Jesus within the story of Israel?

3

The Gospels use teachings, miracles, and people's reactions to reveal Jesus' identity. Why might the authors show us different responses instead of simply telling us what to believe?

4

All four Gospels build toward Jesus' death and resurrection. Why is this the climax of the story rather than simply the tragic ending of Jesus' life?

02

Reflect on the message

Consider what the Gospel asks us to believe and how it challenges the way we see Jesus.

5

The four Gospels sometimes arrange events differently and emphasize different details. How can these differences help us see a fuller picture of Jesus rather than undermine the story?

6

People in the Gospels respond to Jesus with faith, doubt, rejection, and confusion. Which of these responses do you recognize in yourself, and what might be behind it?

7

The Gospels are not meant only to inform us about Jesus; they invite us to follow him. What would it mean to let his story reshape the way you understand God, the world, and yourself?

One thought to carry with you
The Gospels are more than biographies of a remarkable teacher. They announce that Jesus is Israel's promised Messiah and the true King, whose victory comes through death and resurrection. His story calls for a response.
Keep exploring: Read Isaiah 52:7–10, Mark 1:1–15, Luke 24:25–27, and John 20:30–31. Notice how the Gospel connects Jesus to Israel's hope and invites readers to believe in him.

For personal study or group discussion. The questions are open-ended; the aim is thoughtful understanding, not a single perfect answer.

BibleProject · A short study

The Story of the Bible

One unfolding story—from creation and humanity’s choice to Jesus and the hope of a renewed creation.

▶ Watch the video
01

Consider the story

Take a moment with each question. Try to explain the idea in your own words, and think about how the different parts of the story connect.

1

What choice does the tree in the garden represent? Why is that choice so important to the rest of the biblical story?

2

Why does the story focus on Abraham, Sarah, and then the people of Israel? What purpose does God have for calling this family?

3

Humanity repeatedly fails to live in God’s way. How does Jesus respond to this failure, and what makes his way different?

4

What is the new choice offered to humanity through Jesus? What kind of community does this choice invite people to become?

02

Reflect on the message

Move beyond remembering the story. Consider what it might mean for the way we live.

5

Where do you see the temptation to define good and evil for yourself in everyday life? What might it look like to trust God instead?

6

Jesus shows a different kind of strength through serving, loving the poor, and loving enemies. Which of these challenges your usual idea of strength the most? Why?

7

The Bible ends with the hope that evil will be removed and heaven and earth united. If you truly believed this future was coming, how might it change the way you live today?

One thought to carry with you
The Bible is not merely a collection of disconnected stories. It is one unfolding story: God creates, humanity turns away, God remains faithful, Jesus opens a new way, and creation moves toward renewal.
Keep exploring: Read Genesis 1–3, Genesis 12:1–3, and Revelation 21:1–5. Notice how the beginning and ending of the biblical story speak to each other.

For personal study or group discussion. The questions are open-ended; the aim is thoughtful understanding, not a single perfect answer.

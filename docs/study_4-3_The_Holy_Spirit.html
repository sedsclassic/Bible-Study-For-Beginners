BibleProject · A short study

The Holy Spirit

God's life-giving breath—empowering leaders and prophets, promised through a coming king, and poured out on all who follow Jesus.

▶ Watch the video
01

Consider the story

Think about how God's Spirit moves from empowering a few people to renewing an entire people.

1

God's Spirit gives Joseph wisdom, Bezalel craftsmanship, and Joshua leadership. What does this show about the kinds of gifts the Spirit can fill?

2

Even Spirit-empowered leaders and prophets fail, and David begs not to lose God's presence after his own failure. Why does the story keep longing for something more?

3

Isaiah promises a king on whom the Spirit fully rests, and Ezekiel promises a Spirit that gives new hearts. How do these two hopes belong together?

4

At Pentecost, the Spirit is poured out on all of Jesus' followers, not just a chosen few. What changes when God's presence moves from a handful of leaders to an entire people?

02

Reflect on the message

Consider what it means to live with the Spirit as a daily companion rather than a distant idea.

5

When David failed, he ran toward God's presence and asked for a clean heart. When you fall short, do you tend to move toward God or away from him?

6

The Spirit empowered skills as ordinary as wisdom, craft, and administration. Which of your gifts might you have overlooked as being "spiritual"?

7

Ezekiel saw the Spirit breathe life into dry bones. Where in your life or community do you long for new life?

One thought to carry with you
The Spirit is not a distant force but God's own breath—once given to a few, promised through a coming king, and now offered to everyone who belongs to Jesus.
Keep exploring: Read Exodus 31:1–5, Isaiah 11:1–5, Psalm 51:10–12, Ezekiel 37:1–14, and Acts 2:1–21. Notice how the Spirit's work widens from one person at a time to the whole people of God.

For personal study or group discussion. The questions are open-ended; the aim is thoughtful understanding, not a single perfect answer.

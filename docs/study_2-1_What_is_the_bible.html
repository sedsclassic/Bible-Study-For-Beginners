What Is the Bible?

One library, many authors, and one unfolding story. What kind of book is the Bible, and how should we read it?

01

Understanding the Bible

Think about the Bible's structure, its history, and the story it tells.

1

The Bible is a library of different books, not just one ordinary book. How does this change the way we should approach reading it?

2

The Hebrew Scriptures are organized around the Torah, the Prophets, and the Writings. What might this structure help us understand about the relationship between God's instruction, Israel's history, and its hope for the future?

3

Biblical history is told to teach wisdom and give hope, not simply to record events. Why might remembering the past be essential to understanding God's purposes?

4

The Bible has human authors, yet it presents itself as God's word. What does this combination suggest about how God works through people?

02

Reading the Bible today

Consider what this bigger picture means for your faith and everyday life.

5

Jesus understood his life as the continuation and fulfillment of Israel's story. How might seeing the Bible as one connected story change the way you understand Jesus?

6

The Bible uses stories, poetry, wisdom, history, and prophecy to teach. What might we miss if we read every part in exactly the same way?

7

If Scripture is meant to shape how we live, not just what we know, what would it look like to become someone who is truly formed by God's word?

The central idea
The Bible is a unified story told through many books and human authors. Its story of God and Israel leads to Jesus, and invites us not only to understand it, but to be shaped by it.
Keep exploring: Read Luke 24:25–27, Luke 24:44–49, 2 Timothy 3:14–17, and Psalm 1. Consider how these passages help us understand the Bible's story and its purpose.

For personal study or group discussion. The questions are open-ended; the aim is thoughtful understanding, not a single perfect answer.

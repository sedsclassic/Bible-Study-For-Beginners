BibleProject · A short study

Grace

God's generous favor, freely given to those who cannot earn it—and the gift that makes new life possible.

▶ Watch the video
01

Consider the story

Think about how God's generosity meets human failure again and again throughout the Bible.

1

In the Old Testament, grace means favor given freely, as when Noah and Moses find it in God's sight. What does it reveal about God that his favor is a gift rather than a reward?

2

After Israel worships the golden calf, God still renews his covenant and describes himself as gracious and compassionate. Why does the story place grace right after such a serious failure?

3

Paul says we are saved by grace through faith, not by our own effort. How does this overturn the common assumption that we must earn God's approval?

4

The New Testament says grace trains us to turn from wrongdoing, not to ignore it. How can grace be both free and transforming?

02

Reflect on the message

Consider what it means to live as someone who has been given far more than they have earned.

5

Many of us quietly believe we have to earn God's love. Where do you feel the pressure to prove yourself, and what would it mean to rest in a gift?

6

Grace is hardest to accept when we feel most unworthy. What do you need to receive from God today without trying to repay it?

7

People who receive grace are called to extend it. Who in your life is waiting for the kind of generosity you have been shown?

One thought to carry with you
Grace is not God lowering the standard but God giving himself freely—meeting us in our failure, and then making us new.
Keep exploring: Read Exodus 33:12–17, Exodus 34:4–7, Romans 5:6–11, Ephesians 2:1–10, and Titus 2:11–14. Notice how grace is always a gift first, and then the power for a changed life.

For personal study or group discussion. The questions are open-ended; the aim is thoughtful understanding, not a single perfect answer.

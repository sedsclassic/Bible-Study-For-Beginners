BibleProject · A short study

Holiness

God's unique goodness and "otherness"—and his invitation for a people to be set apart as his own.

▶ Watch the video
01

Consider the story

Think about what it means that God is holy and how that shapes his relationship with his people.

1

In the Bible, "holy" means set apart, belonging to God in a way nothing else does. What does it mean that God is holy in a way that nothing in creation can match?

2

Isaiah sees heavenly beings crying out "holy, holy, holy" and is overwhelmed by his own unworthiness. Why does meeting God's holiness expose human brokenness?

3

God calls Israel to be holy because he is holy, setting them apart through the tabernacle, priesthood, and law. What was this set-apart life meant to show the nations?

4

In Jesus, God's holiness reaches toward the unclean instead of retreating from them. How does this change our picture of what holiness looks like?

02

Reflect on the message

Consider what it means to belong to a God who is both awe-inspiring and near.

5

Biblical holiness is less about rule-keeping than about belonging to God. What would it look like to be set apart for him in your ordinary life?

6

Where do you tend to treat holiness as a list of restrictions instead of an invitation into God's life? How might that view need to change?

7

God's holiness is both overwhelming and welcoming. How does holding both truths together change the way you come to him?

One thought to carry with you
God's holiness is not coldness or distance. It is his unique goodness and love, which does not shun the broken but sets them apart to belong to him.
Keep exploring: Read Exodus 19:3–6, Leviticus 19:1–2, Isaiah 6:1–8, Mark 1:40–45, and 1 Peter 1:13–16. Notice how God's call to be holy becomes an invitation to share in his own life.

For personal study or group discussion. The questions are open-ended; the aim is thoughtful understanding, not a single perfect answer.

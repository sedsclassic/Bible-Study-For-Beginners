BibleProject · A short study

Satan

The accuser who tempts humanity to doubt God's goodness—from the serpent in Eden to his defeat through Jesus.

▶ Watch the video
01

Consider the story

Think about how the Bible portrays the adversary and what it says about who truly holds power.

1

In Eden, the serpent doesn't attack God openly but plants doubt about his goodness. Why is questioning God's character such a powerful form of temptation?

2

In Job and Zechariah, "the satan" appears as an accuser who operates under God's authority. What does this reveal about the limits of his power?

3

In the wilderness, Jesus faces the same tests humanity failed, shaping what loyalty to God looks like. What does his refusal to take shortcuts show about true victory?

4

Revelation describes the accuser being thrown down through the death of Jesus and the faithfulness of his followers. How can the cross be a defeat of evil rather than a defeat for Jesus?

02

Reflect on the message

Consider how evil works in the ordinary places of life and where your hope rests.

5

The adversary's strategy begins with whispered doubts about God's goodness. Where do you most often find yourself questioning whether God is truly for you?

6

Accusation tries to define people by their worst moments, while Jesus speaks forgiveness and belonging. Which voice do you listen to most when you fail?

7

Evil is real, but Scripture says it is already defeated and will be undone. How does that confidence change the way you face fear and spiritual struggle?

One thought to carry with you
Evil is real, but it is not equal to God. Jesus overcomes the accuser through faithful love and sacrifice, and his victory is the ground we stand on.
Keep exploring: Read Genesis 3:1–15, Job 1:6–12, Zechariah 3:1–5, Matthew 4:1–11, and Revelation 12:7–11. Notice how the accuser's voice is answered again and again by God's faithfulness.

For personal study or group discussion. The questions are open-ended; the aim is thoughtful understanding, not a single perfect answer.
